# Web Research Notes

Selection logic:

- I prioritized layouts that preserve a clear reading order and avoid heavy template machinery.
- I treated official career-service ATS advice as the constraint layer.
- I used template galleries as inspiration for layout shapes, not as material to reproduce verbatim.

Sources:

- [University of Pennsylvania resume guidance](https://careerservices.upenn.edu/channels/resume/): Clear ATS guidance: simple, consistent design; use white space; avoid graphics and text boxes.
- [Yale resume action verbs](https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/resume-action-verbs/): Useful for stronger bullets focused on accomplishment rather than duty language.
- [Europass CV guidance](https://europass.europa.eu/en/create-europass-cv): Supports a Europe-familiar structure, reverse chronological order, and tailored About Me framing.
- [Overleaf MTeck's Resume](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/templates/mtecks-resume/fzgztpkgngjc): Strong single-column ATS-oriented pattern with simple, maintainable styling.
- [Overleaf CV Sidebar Template](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/templates/cv-sidebar-template/mnwdwhxbxgdg): Shows a lightweight sidebar pattern that still stays visually clean.
- [Overleaf Monocol Navbar CV](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/templates/monocol-navbar-cv/xdhwjpkpmxyv): Shows a compact left-rail contact pattern that feels modern without heavy layout logic.

Result:

- The best code-light options are still a disciplined single-column base, a restrained sidebar, a simple left rail, and a Europass-style information grid.
